5 Nutrition Myths Worth Dropping

By Gelila

Nutrition advice is everywhere — and a lot of it doesn't hold up. Here are five common rules I hear constantly from clients, and why it's okay to let them go.

1. "Carbs are bad for you"

Carbohydrates are your body's preferred energy source. Whole grains, fruit, and legumes come packed with fiber, vitamins, and minerals your body actually needs. The issue was never carbs themselves — it's the balance of what's on your plate.

2. "You have to eat 6 small meals a day"

There's no strong evidence that meal frequency affects metabolism the way this myth suggests. Some people do well with 3 meals, others with 2 or 5 — what matters more is that your overall pattern fits your life and keeps you satisfied.

3. "Detox teas and cleanses remove toxins"

Your liver and kidneys already do this job continuously. No tea, juice, or supplement speeds that process up — and many "cleanses" just cause water loss and digestive discomfort, not fat loss or "toxin removal."

4. "Eating after 8pm causes weight gain"

Your body doesn't have an internal clock that turns food into fat after a certain hour. What matters is your total intake and quality of food across the day, not the exact time it's eaten.

5. "Healthy eating means cutting out entire food groups"

Unless there's a medical reason (an allergy, intolerance, or diagnosed condition), restricting entire food groups usually isn't necessary — and it often makes eating harder to sustain, not easier.
